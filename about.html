Our Origin Story

Three Generations of
Flour on Our Hands.

How a weekend market stall became one of Sandton's most requested bakeries.

Lelatela lamola learned to bake sourdough from his grandmother's handwritten notebook, long before "artisan" was a word anyone put on a shop sign. In 2025, she started selling loaves out of a fold-out table at the Sandton weekend market — three varieties, cash only, gone by 10am most Saturdays.

What began as a stall became a small crew, then a storefront at Hatfield, then a waitlist for custom cakes that now stretches months into the future. The name stuck as an inside joke that never stopped being funny: "the Kartel" — because once you're in, you don't really leave.

"We don't do trends. We do the recipe that works, made properly, every single time."
— lelatela lamola, Founder

Today the Kartel bakes well over a thousand items a week — from the daily sourdough to full wedding showpieces — and every single one still gets tasted by the crew before it goes out the door.
